## What Kind of Game Is Terrible Lizards?

Terrible Lizards is a horror game with first-person parkour at its core. You are not a fighter. You are a runner, a climber, and a survivor. The creatures that stalk the abandoned dinosaur attraction shamble and then charge, and the only reliable answer is movement — jumping, sliding, wall running, bouncing off surfaces, and chaining momentum until you're out of reach.

The developers describe the movement as intuitive to learn but deep to master, which is a familiar promise in the parkour genre and an important one here: if the traversal doesn't feel good, a chase-driven horror game falls apart. Early footage and the playable demo suggest the emphasis is on fluid, weighty motion rather than floaty platforming.

### The Story: Dalton's Dinos, 1992

The setup is deliberately retro. Dalton's Dinos was once a must-see Route 66 roadside attraction, baking under the New Mexico sun. Now the staff have vanished, and a voice on the telephone you find inside is begging you to help locate a missing child. Your car died miles from anywhere, it's 1992, and there are no cell phones or rideshares coming to rescue you. So you go in.

That time period is doing real work. A 1992 setting removes every modern convenience that would normally solve a horror plot in thirty seconds, and it lets the art team lean into faded roadside Americana — cracked fiberglass dinosaurs, sun-bleached signs, and the sense that something is buried underneath all of it.

### No Weapons, All Momentum

According to the developer's own feature breakdown, combat is explicitly not an option. You outsmart and outmaneuver your pursuers instead. That design choice shapes everything:

- **Chase pressure over resource management:** You're not counting bullets, you're counting seconds.
- **Environmental storytelling:** Tapes, notes, and recordings carry the narrative, so exploration is rewarded rather than punished.
- **Atmospheric dread:** The Steam tags read like a checklist of modern horror — psychological horror, survival horror, supernatural, story-rich, and 1990s.
- **Fast-paced pacing:** Between the platformer and action-adventure tags, expect short bursts of terror rather than long, quiet stretches.

Community reports from the demo suggest the tension comes less from jump scares and more from the constant knowledge that something is behind you. Treat that as player experience rather than a guarantee — tastes vary, and the full game may pace itself differently.
