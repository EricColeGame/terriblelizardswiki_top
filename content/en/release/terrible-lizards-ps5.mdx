## Story and Setting: Dalton's Dinos in 1992

The premise is tightly wound and deliberately retro. You arrive at Dalton's Dinos, a once-famous roadside attraction baking under the New Mexico sun along Route 66. The staff are gone. Your car has died miles from anywhere. It is 1992 — no cell phones, no rideshare apps, no cavalry on the way.

The only thread pulling you deeper is a voice on a telephone inside the attraction, pleading for help finding a missing child. From there, the game escalates into a survival chase through a crumbling monument to mid-century Americana, with something far worse waiting beneath it.

The 1992 setting is doing real work here, not just decorating the screen. A pre-internet, pre-smartphone world removes the two crutches that modern horror games lean on most: calling for help and looking up answers. There is no map app, no flashlight phone, no backup. That isolation is the engine of the tension.

## Gameplay: Horror Meets First-Person Parkour

### The Movement Toolkit

Understanding your verbs is the fastest way to improve. The game asks you to chain movement options rather than use them one at a time, so think in combos, not buttons.

| Move | Best Used For |
|---|---|
| Run | Covering open ground and building speed |
| Jump | Clearing gaps and broken structures |
| Climb | Reaching vertical routes and escape ledges |
| Wall run | Cutting corners and bypassing chokepoints |
| Bounce | Redirecting momentum off surfaces |
| Slide | Slipping under obstacles and dropping speed fast |

Player experience with momentum-based movement systems generally shows the same pattern: beginners panic and mash, while veterans plan two or three moves ahead. If you can internalize one rule before launch, make it this — never stop moving in a straight line when a wall is nearby.

## Tips to Survive Your First Night at Dalton's Dinos

Because there is no combat, survival is entirely about movement economy. Here is how to approach your first run.

- **Learn the map before you need it.** Exploration is cheap when nothing is chasing you and priceless when something is.
- **Never commit to a dead end.** Any route that ends in a wall is a coffin. Scan for a second exit before you enter a room.
- **Chain moves, do not stack them.** A wall run into a jump keeps speed; a wall run into a full stop does not.
- **Use vertical space.** Climbing and bouncing exist because the ground floor is where the creatures live.
- **Listen first, move second.** Audio cues are your early warning system, and captions can back them up.
- **Collect everything.** Tapes, notes, and recordings are the story. Skipping them means missing why any of this is happening.
- **Pause when you need to.** The pause feature is there for a reason. Use it to breathe, not to quit.

| Common Mistake | Better Approach |
|---|---|
| Sprinting in a straight line | Break line of sight with corners and walls |
| Backing into a corner to think | Keep at least two exits in view |
| Ignoring audio cues | Treat sound as your first warning |
| Hoarding speed in tight spaces | Slide or bounce to redirect instead |
| Skipping collectibles | They explain the mystery and reward exploration |
