## Gameplay Breakdown: Horror, Parkour, and Zero Weapons

Terrible Lizards leans hard into momentum. Sprinting, vaulting, sliding, and climbing chain together, and the game rewards players who keep moving rather than stopping to fight. Since there is no combat system, your only defensive tools are speed, route knowledge, and distance.

Worth noting up front: the Terrible Lizards secrets rarely sit directly on a chase line. They are tucked into side rooms, upper walkways, and service areas, which means the safest time to collect them is when nothing is actively hunting you.

| Movement tool | Best used for | Risk if mistimed |
|---|---|---|
| Sprint | Crossing open ground | Burns stamina and noise budget |
| Vault | Clearing fences and railings | Slow recovery if you clip geometry |
| Slide | Ducking under obstacles | Hard to cancel mid-animation |
| Climb | Reaching rooftops and service areas | Leaves you visible while ascending |

Two habits separate confident runs from panicked ones. First, chain moves instead of tapping them individually — the movement system is built around flow, and stuttering breaks your rhythm. Second, learn to read sound. Footsteps, creaks, and distant screeches telegraph pursuit long before the creature is on screen.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Keep two exits in mind for every room | Back into a corridor you have not scouted |
| Use vertical space to break line of sight | Assume higher ground is automatically safe |
| Pause in cover and listen | Sprint everywhere and drain your stamina |
| Re-walk routes after a failed chase | Restart the whole run over one mistake |
