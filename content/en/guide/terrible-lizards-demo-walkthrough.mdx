## Step-by-Step Terrible Lizards Demo Walkthrough Route

Use this sequence whenever you restart. It keeps the opening manageable without assuming that every landmark carries the same danger level.

1. **Survey the entrance.** Stop before moving deep into the attraction. Look for the widest route, the visible display structures, and any direction that offers a clean retreat. Avoid entering a narrow area before you understand where it leads.
2. **Build a landmark chain.** Pick three or four memorable objects — a sign, a specific dinosaur figure, a building — and memorize their order. A short, reliable chain beats trying to remember every decoration.
3. **Check each display before passing.** Approach large figures and themed structures from an angle that keeps the surrounding space visible. Pause after each movement instead of crossing several landmarks at once.
4. **Preserve a retreat lane.** Before investigating anything suspicious, confirm the route behind you is still usable. Scenery, walls, and tight turns can quietly remove your only obvious escape direction.
5. **Advance in short sections.** Break the demo into small exploration segments. After each one, reassess what changed, where danger could be hiding, and whether continuing beats repositioning.

The route should feel deliberate rather than cautious for its own sake. A dinosaur attraction naturally creates visual noise, and the demo rewards players who can tell decoration apart from useful information.

## Exploration Priorities: Sightlines, Retreat Lanes, and Landmarks

When two paths look equally viable, the tiebreaker is almost always readability. A route you understand is worth more than a route that is merely shorter.

### Visibility

Prefer paths where displays, turns, and open ground stay readable. Visibility buys reaction time, and reaction time is the only real currency in a demo like this.

### Retreat Space

Keep a clear route behind you. A path is safer when you can leave it without making a blind turn into cluttered scenery.

### Landmarks

Favor memorable objects that help you track position and recognize when you are drifting deeper than intended.

### Pacing

Move in controlled bursts. Pausing after an uncertain sound, a flicker of movement, or a blocked sightline prevents careless commitments.

| Situation | Risk Level | Recommended Response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wide route with several visible exits | Lower | Continue while checking landmarks |
| Large display blocking the next turn | Moderate | Reposition before advancing |
| Narrow passage with limited visibility | High | Enter slowly or find another angle |
| Unfamiliar area without landmarks | High | Pause and identify a return route |
| Suspected creature zone | Very high | Observe, keep space, avoid blind movement |

A practical rule: leave yourself one decision before danger, not zero. If you wait until an encounter is unavoidable, the environment has already taken control of your options.

## Practical Tips for the Route 66 Attraction

The Route 66 theme encourages curiosity, but curiosity should be controlled. Treat every visual attraction as both a landmark and a possible blind spot. That mindset lets you enjoy the setting while staying ready for the demo's prehistoric danger.

- **Use edges carefully.** The side of a route may improve visibility, but it can also reduce your turning space.
- **Avoid landmark overload.** Memorize a few major objects rather than every piece of scenery.
- **Pause after transitions.** Turns, display entrances, and shifts from open ground to narrow paths all deserve a quick scan.
- **Do not backtrack blindly.** Recheck the route behind you before retreating through cluttered areas.
- **Choose information over speed.** If two paths look equal, take the one that reveals more of the surrounding area.
- **Treat quiet as uncertain.** Silence does not confirm that a display zone is safe.
- **Keep the route simple.** A clear mental map beats a complicated sequence of tiny turns.

| Tip | Use It When | Expected Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pause at turns | Visibility changes suddenly | Fewer blind commitments |
| Mark large displays | The attraction has repeated scenery | Easier route recognition |
| Keep open ground nearby | Entering an uncertain section | More room to reposition |
| Recheck the approach | Returning from a deeper zone | Less chance of losing direction |

If an area feels hard to read, step back and approach it from a different angle. The goal is not to force the first available route — it is to make the environment understandable enough that your next decision is deliberate. Community reports consistently point to the same pattern: the players who slow down early end up moving faster later, because they stop relearning the same mistakes.
