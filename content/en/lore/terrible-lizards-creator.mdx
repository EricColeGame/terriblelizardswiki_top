## Why "Terrible Lizards"? The Name's Double Meaning

The title is doing more work than it first appears. "Terrible lizards" is a near-literal translation of *Dinosauria*, the group name coined by British anatomist Richard Owen in 1842 from the Greek *deinos* ("terrible" or "fearfully great") and *sauros* ("lizard").

| Term | Origin | Literal Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Dinosauria | Coined by Richard Owen, 1842 | "Terrible lizards" |
| Deinos | Ancient Greek | Terrible, fearful, wondrous |
| Sauros | Ancient Greek | Lizard, reptile |

That etymology gives the Terrible Lizards creator a title that works on two levels. On one hand, it is a wink at dinosaur fiction — the classic monster-park setup. On the other, it is a promise: these are the *terrible* lizards, not the friendly ones on the brochure. The title tells you the attraction's mascots are not on your side.
