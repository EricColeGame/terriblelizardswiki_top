## The Core Movement Kit: Every Move in the Terrible Lizards Parkour System

Six verbs do all the work. The creatures "shamble and charge," which means they're relentless but not agile — your entire advantage comes from verticality, corners, and momentum you refuse to give up.

| Move | What it does | Best used for | What it costs you |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Run / Sprint | Builds and holds forward speed | Open lanes, straight corridors | Wide turns scrub speed fast |
| Jump | Clears gaps and low geometry | Broken walkways, debris fields | Landing mid-chase stalls your rhythm |
| Climb | Scales ledges, pipes, and vertical faces | Shortcuts out of dead ends | You're slow and exposed while doing it |
| Wall Run | Carries speed along a flat vertical surface | Narrow gaps, shafts, tight hallways | Requires a clean entry angle |
| Bounce | Redirects momentum off a surface | Chaining movement without stopping | Mistimed input throws away your speed |
| Slide | Low profile, fast direction change | Passing under barriers, killing corners | Short recovery window after |

The important thing is that these aren't six separate tools — they're links in a chain. A wall run that ends in a jump that ends in a slide is one continuous motion. Break the chain and the creature closes the distance.

## Momentum and Routing: How a Chase Actually Plays Out

Chases in this game follow a rhythm. Because the creatures both shamble (slow, stalking) and charge (fast, committed), a pursuit tends to move through recognizable phases. Treat this as a framework for planning routes, not a script — the exact behavior depends on the encounter.

| Phase | What the creature is doing | What you should be doing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Notice | Shambling, scanning, reacting to noise | Break line of sight before it commits — don't wait to confirm |
| Commit | Charging in an aggressive, mostly straight line | Pick the route that gives you a vertical exit, not the shortest one |
| Pressure | Holding pace, cutting corners, staying close | Chain moves continuously; never stop to look back |
| Reset | Gap opens, audio quiets down | Recover, re-read the room, plan the next two links |

Routing is where most runs are won. The fastest line is rarely the straightest one — it's the one that keeps you moving at all times. Use this decision table when you're mid-panic:

| Situation | Recommended move | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Long straight hallway, creature behind | Sprint | Pure speed, no geometry required |
| Dead end with a low ledge | Climb, then jump | Vertical is the only exit left |
| Narrow gap between two walls | Wall run | Keeps momentum without touching the floor |
| Collapsed floor or drop ahead | Bounce into a jump | Converts downward motion into distance |
| Low beam, sign, or barrier | Slide | Fastest way through without slowing |
| Sharp corner at full speed | Slide into sprint | Preserves far more speed than a hard turn |

One practical rule: enter every unfamiliar room at half speed. You can always accelerate once you know where the exits are, but you can't un-run into a dead end.

## Practice Drills and the Mistakes That Break Your Flow

The demo is the ideal training ground because the stakes are low and the layout is learnable. A structured loop beats aimless sprinting.

| Drill | Goal | How to run it | Time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Warm-up walk | Learn the space | Tour the attraction with no pressure, note every exit | 10 min |
| Speed lines | Memorize sprint lanes | Run one route repeatedly until it's boring | 15 min |
| Vertical link | Chain climb into wall run | Pick a single shaft and repeat the sequence | 10 min |
| Corner work | Slide out of turns | Loop one sharp corner until it's automatic | 10 min |
| Full escape | Combine everything | One continuous run, start to finish | 20 min |

Then audit your failures. These are the mistakes that show up most often in momentum-driven chase games, and each one has a cheap fix:

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Looking back at the creature | Kills forward momentum and blinds your route | Use audio cues instead of turning |
| Jumping too early | You land short and stall | Jump at the lip of the gap, not before it |
| Hard turns at full speed | Wipes out your accumulated speed | Slide into the turn, then sprint out |
| Climbing when you don't need to | Slows you down in the open | Climb only when it opens a new route |
| Trying to fight | There are no weapons — combat isn't an option | Outmaneuver, don't engage |
| Sprinting into unknown rooms | You run straight into a dead end | Half speed on first entry, full speed on the way out |

Because the game tracks Steam Achievements and runs as a single-player experience, your progress is entirely on you. There's no co-op partner to bail you out and no matchmaking to blame.

## FAQ

**Is Terrible Lizards parkour hard to learn?**
The input list is small — run, jump, climb, wall run, bounce, slide — so the basics click quickly. The difficulty is in stringing them together under pressure while a creature charges you. Expect the learning curve to be about composure as much as execution.

**Do I need to fight the creatures at any point?**
No. The game is explicit that there are no weapons and combat is not an option. Every encounter is resolved by movement: breaking line of sight, taking a vertical route, or simply outrunning the charge.

**Should I play the demo before the full release?**
Yes. The demo has been available since September 24, 2026, and the full game arrives October 29, 2026. Practicing routes now means you'll spend launch day exploring the mystery instead of re-learning how to wall run.

**What's the best way to improve at Terrible Lizards parkour fast?**
Repeat single routes instead of improvising. Memorize one speed line, one vertical link, and one corner, then combine them into a full escape run. Muscle memory beats reaction time every time.

The bottom line: this is a game about never stopping. Learn the six moves, protect your momentum, and route every room before you sprint through it — and Dalton's Dinos becomes a puzzle you can solve at speed.
